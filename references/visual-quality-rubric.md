# Visual quality rubric

Score rendered UI, not source code or intention. Use ten dimensions, each from 1–5:

1. Visual Identity
2. Hierarchy
3. Composition
4. Spacing Rhythm
5. Typography
6. Color Discipline
7. Imagery Consistency
8. Component Coherence
9. Platform Appropriateness
10. Memorability

## Scale

- **1 — Failure:** visibly broken or contradicts the approved direction.
- **2 — Weak:** usable in places, but visually unresolved or generic.
- **3 — Acceptable:** coherent and serviceable, with notable limitations.
- **4 — Strong:** evidently designed, consistent, and purposeful.
- **5 — Mature:** highly resolved, distinctive, and robust under real content.

Add the ten values for a total out of 50 and multiply by two for a score out of 100.

- 90–100: Excellent
- 80–89: Strong
- 70–79: Acceptable
- 60–69: Weak
- below 60: Must Iterate

Default Gate C threshold is 80. Key screens require at least 85; hero, home, and core-decision screens require at least 88 unless the approved project threshold is stricter.

## Scoring discipline

For every dimension cite visible evidence and one improvement when below 4. A functional success cannot raise the visual score. A striking screen with platform or accessibility failure cannot receive a 5 in Platform Appropriateness. Record the dimension values, total, threshold, top three problems, critical issues, and re-render decision.
