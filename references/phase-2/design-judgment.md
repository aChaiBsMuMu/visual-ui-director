# Design judgment

Analysis describes; judgment decides. Every consequential decision receives a J-ID, references finding IDs and records: alternatives, selected behavior, rejected alternative, fit, tradeoff, rule, failure signal and provenance.

Valid grounds: Product Need, User Need, Primary Reference, Approved Secondary Contribution, Platform Constraint, Accessibility Requirement or Explicit Proposed Decision. A Proposed Decision must explain its hypothesis and verification; it is not permission for arbitrary decoration. Identify which evidence could reverse the decision.

Translate adjectives into behavior. “高级 / premium” could mean low competing accent frequency, deliberate material boundaries and strong type contrast for this product; “科技感 / technical” could mean aligned numeric columns and immediate state feedback; “温暖 / warm” could mean subject-focused human imagery and softer surface contrast. These are examples to test, not universal translations. “Modern apps do this”, “more beautiful”, “elegant” and “looks premium” cannot be the sole rationale.

Judge fit against actual users, task, content, platform, accessibility and asset availability. A watch's short glance can outweigh editorial expression; native navigation behavior can win while brand color and image crop remain expressive. Explicitly decide containment, density, hierarchy, accent competition and which signature gesture deserves a limited exception.

Each important RULE in analysis must end in an adopted/adapted/rejected judgment; each major manual decision must trace back through strategy to at least one legitimate source. Keep causal detail here; strategy summarizes final choices, rather than repeating every observation.
